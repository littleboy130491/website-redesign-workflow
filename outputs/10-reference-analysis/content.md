# Reference Analysis: Content Perspective

**References Analyzed:** Pew Research Center, Law.com
**Screenshots:** outputs/06-reference-screenshots/content/

## Key Content Patterns

### 1. Headline & Value Proposition

**Pew Research Center**
- Clear, descriptive title: "Nonpartisan, nonadvocacy, public opinion polling and data-driven social science research"
- Immediately communicates mission and differentiation
- No marketing fluff — factual and authoritative
- Subheadlines expand without repeating

**Law.com**
- Industry-focused messaging
- Clear audience identification (legal professionals)
- Authority signals through industry naming

### 2. Content Structure

**Pew Research Patterns:**
- Clear topical navigation (menu items describe content areas)
- Homepage features latest research with thumbnails
- Topic pages with clear hierarchy
- Article pages have: headline, byline, date, body, related links
- Data visualizations embedded in content
- Sources cited clearly

**Law.com Patterns:**
- News-style layout with timestamps
- Category-based organization
- "Trending" or "Most Read" sections
- Email signup prompts

### 3. Tone & Voice

**Authoritative Legal Content:**
- Professional but accessible language
- Factual, evidence-based presentation
- Jargon explained when used
- Clear attribution and sourcing
- No hyperbolic marketing language

**Recommended Tone for Law Firm:**
- Professional and authoritative
- Indonesian language with proper legal terminology
- Approachable but serious
- Trust-building through expertise demonstration
- Client-focused (not self-promotional)

### 4. Social Proof Placement

**Effective Patterns:**
- "Featured in" logos early on page
- Client testimonials with photos and names
- Case studies with measurable outcomes
- Partner/firm credentials near CTAs
- Industry recognition badges

### 5. Call-to-Action Phrasing

**Weak phrases to avoid:**
- "Contact us" (generic)
- "Learn more" (vague)
- "Click here" (lazy)

**Strong phrases observed:**
- "Schedule a Consultation"
- "Speak with an Expert"
- "Get a Free Case Evaluation"
- "Request More Information"
- "Download Our Guide"

### 6. Content Hierarchy

**Homepage Content Order (Recommended):**
1. Hero with value proposition
2. Core services (3-4 bullets)
3. Why choose us (differentiators)
4. Social proof (testimonials/logos)
5. Featured content (articles/case studies)
6. Contact CTA
7. Footer with navigation and contact

## Recommendations for Redesign

1. **Value Proposition** - Add clear "What we do" / "Who we serve" statement on homepage
2. **Service Descriptions** - Use Pew Research-style clear, factual language
3. **Bylines** - Add author attribution on all articles
4. **CTA Language** - Use "Schedule a Consultation" not "Contact Us"
5. **Social Proof** - Add testimonials section and client credential display
6. **Article Structure** - Include date, author, estimated read time, related links
